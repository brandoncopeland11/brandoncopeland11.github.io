In-app collection ads

Completed while at Flipp

Overview

In-App Collection Ads transformed Flipp's ad experience by replacing legacy, static Google Ad Manager (GAM) banners with a dynamic, video/display unit paired with an interactive 1 to 3 item shoppable carousel.

As the lead product designer for this initiative, I led the end-to-end design strategy across two high-traffic app surfaces: Search and Browse. My focus was on driving higher ad engagement and CPM value for brand partners while establishing strict UX guardrails to protect core flyer browsing, user retention, and overall ecosystem health.

What is Flipp?

Flipp is a leading deal-discovery platform that helps millions of shoppers plan weekly grocery and retail purchases through digital flyers, search, and personalized deal feeds from local retailers.

Problem

Flipp needed to move beyond legacy static Google Ad Manager (GAM) banners. They were reliable for revenue, but engagement was weak—a baseline CTR around 0.29%—and they couldn't showcase actionable, item-level deals directly inside the feed.

The core design challenge was introducing high-impact, shoppable Collection Ads into Search and Browse without degrading flyer browsing, session depth, or retention. On Browse especially, new ad units would compete for feed real estate alongside premium digital flyer tiles—the product experience users come to Flipp for.

Goals

  • Business: Increase ad engagement and CPM value for brand partners through a native video and shoppable carousel format.
  • User: Protect core flyer browsing and retention; ads should feel integrated into the feed, not disruptive to it.
  • Design: Ship a cohesive Collection Ads experience across Search (high-intent) and Browse (discovery-first) with surface-specific UX patterns.
  • Guardrails: Define ad system rules—slot placement, density, video behavior, and carousel interactions— before scaling into high-traffic feed positions.

The solution

Collection Ads replace static banners with a dynamic video or display unit paired with an interactive 1–3 item shoppable carousel—a modern, CPM-billed format designed to feel native on each surface while giving brand partners higher-impact placement.

The format launched across two primary surfaces:

  • Search: Positioned at slots 3 and 7 within Search results. Targeted by exact, competitive, and related keywords, this surface served high-intent shoppers and proved stable early on.
  • Browse: Placed within high-value feed slots (slots 4–9), competing directly for real estate alongside Flipp's primary content—premium digital flyer tiles.

User research

Because ads would ship into feed slots competing with core flyer content, we couldn't scale on intuition alone. Before launching across Search and Browse, Design and Research ran 7 unmoderated rounds via UserTesting.com with 84 participants—casual, power, and browser personas—to validate engagement without harming retention.

We tested video controls, item-card interactions, carousel card density, and alternative IAB formats (250×250 and 300×250).

Outcome

  • 📈

    A native ad product replaced legacy banners

    Collection Ads moved Flipp from static GAM banners to a CPM-billed, in-feed format with video or display plus a shoppable 1–3 item carousel—giving brand partners item-level storytelling the legacy unit couldn't support.

  • 🔍

    Search launched and scaled first

    Search placements at slots 3 and 7 shipped ahead of Browse and proved stable with high-intent shoppers—creating a validated foundation before expanding into the more sensitive Browse feed.

  • 🛡️

    Research-backed guardrails shipped

    Seven rounds of testing across 84 participants codified core rules: muted video by default, carousel density limits, surface-specific slot placement, and tighter Browse footprints to reduce feed disruption.

  • 🛒

    Shoppable carousel drove engagement

    Users interacted with the carousel when deals were visually clear—red pricing and sale cues made the unit feel relevant rather than purely promotional, aligning ad value with Flipp's deal-discovery mission.

  • 🧭

    Browse expanded with retention guardrails intact

    Browse rolled out to high-value feed slots (4–9) with stricter visual and placement standards informed by Visual Impact and format testing—balancing monetization in direct competition with premium flyer tiles while keeping the feed usable for core shopping behavior.

Design

Research findings directly shaped the shipped UI—slot placement, video behavior, carousel density, and surface-specific layouts for Search and Browse. Each variant was designed to feel native in-feed while making deals scannable at a glance.

On Search, the unit paired video or display with a shoppable carousel beneath, optimized for high-intent keyword context at slots 3 and 7. Browse used a tighter footprint for slots 4–9, with item cards tuned for faster scanning so the ad didn't compete visually with premium flyer tiles.

  • Video: Autoplay muted by default, with explicit controls—addressing the top retention concern from testing.
  • Carousel: 1–3 item slots with sale-forward pricing (red price, deal cues) to match how users already scan Flipp feeds.
  • Guardrails: Sponsored labeling, density caps, and surface-specific slot rules codified into the ad system before Browse scaled.
