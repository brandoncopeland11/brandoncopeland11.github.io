Add to list UX case study

Completed while at Flipp

Overview

This project aimed to make adding deals and products to the shopping list clearer, more consistent, and easier to act on, so users could save items while browsing and actually return to shop from their list.

57% of MAU add items to their list, but only 38% navigate to the shopping list, revealing a disconnect. Users struggled with add-to-list awareness, unclear feedback after saving, and finding added items in a busy list.

The greatest opportunity was converting engaged visits into EV (engaged visit) + add to list sessions by encouraging users to add items during shopping sessions and making the relationship between saved items and the shopping list more intuitive.

What is Flipp?

Flipp is a shopping platform that lets users discover deals, coupons, and flyers from local retailers to save money on products.

Problem

Adding items to a list across the app can be challenging. While it seems straightforward, users often struggle to complete the action successfully.

The add interaction lacked clarity, failed to show where items went after being added, and created inconsistencies that hindered effective shopping list management.

Goal

  • Increase add-to-list awareness: enhance visibility and discoverability of add to list functionality across the app.
  • Increase consistency: align the add to list experience across search, in-flyer, and browse so users encounter the same patterns no matter how they save an item.
  • Provide clear feedback: introduce user feedback that clearly indicates where added items are saved.
  • Strengthen user understanding: educate users on the connection between add to list and their shopping list through intuitive design and prompts.

Process

The process moved through define, research, ideate, test, refine, and ship. To assess the significant design changes, I conducted two rounds of unmoderated user testing.

This approach provided valuable insights and confidence for further refinement and development.

Outcome

  • ✅

    Strong task completion

    Adding from search results was 100% successful across both rounds (20/20, then 10/10), with average satisfaction of 6.8–6.9 out of 7. The in-flyer task improved from 90% (18/20) in round one to 100% (10/10) after iteration.

  • ✨

    Visual feedback built confidence

    Users praised the animation, checkmark, and number badge for confirming adds. Most noticed the item animation move into the List icon on the first try, and the number badge beside the List icon became a key signal that an item was saved.

  • ↩️

    Removing items was intuitive

    All participants identified ways to remove items from their list, whether by deselecting in the flyer, using the checkmark, or navigating to the list. In round two, most users spotted multiple removal paths without help.

  • 🚀

    Round two refinements worked

    The “Add to list” button, item animation, and quantity selector were well understood. Users expanded the bottom sheet instinctively, found Share, and the move to “Shop Now” helped separate adding to a list from shopping on the retailer’s site.

  • 💡

    Opportunities to refine further

    Some users confused the List icon with cart or “See it” actions, a few preferred reviewing before adding rather than one-click adds, and clearer bottom sheet language could reduce overlap between list-building and shopping flows.

UX explorations

The UX explored clearer add states, item quantity controls, list-level feedback, and confirmation patterns that help users understand when items have been added successfully.

Reflections

  • Initially, we considered a full revamp of the swipe and flyer viewing experience. However, exploring various options showed that a redesign was not essential. Instead, I leveraged native components and common practices to solve the problem.
  • I was confident skipping usability testing before the experiment due to the use of common design practices and components, which positively impacted development time and user interaction.
