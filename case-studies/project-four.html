Flyer swiping (9% growth adoption)

Completed while at Flipp

Overview

This project aims to improve the in-flyer "Next Flyer" experience, enhancing navigation and driving up to $1MM in revenue through optimized cross-browse recommendations.

The focus was on helping shoppers better understand that more flyers were available to swipe through, while making the recommendation experience clearer and more predictable across devices.

The final solution introduced swipe education, a next flyer peek, and a pagination controller to make cross-browse navigation easier to discover and more consistent.

What is Flipp?

Flipp is a shopping platform that lets users discover deals, coupons, and flyers from local retailers to save money on products.

Problem

Flipp's flyer recommendation experience, where shoppers spend 71% of their session time and generate 13-14% of ad clicks, had not been updated in 5 years.

Before this work, transitions between flyers felt inconsistent and gave users little signal that more flyers were available, leading to failed navigation and missed engagement opportunities that limited usability and ad performance.

Goals

Goal

Improve awareness

Enhance the in-flyer "Next Flyer" swiping experience by improving user awareness and understanding of the swiping functionality.

Improve flyer predictability and relevancy per user.

Process

The work moved through define, ideate, refine, test, and ship. Initially, we considered a full revamp of the swipe and flyer viewing experience. Exploring various options showed that a redesign was not essential, so I leveraged native components and common practices to solve the problem.

To evaluate the impact of the new designs, we ran a 3-month experiment with a small test group in the app. This gave us confidence in making changes to a risky area. I was confident skipping usability testing before the experiment because the solution relied on common design practices and shared components, which positively impacted development time and user interaction.

Design

To increase user swiping awareness, we introduced a next flyer "peek" to indicate that more flyers were available to swipe to. This was paired with a swipe indication pill to further communicate the next flyer functionality.

A common component, the pagination controller, was also implemented to improve visibility of other flyers in the cross-browse carousel. Although the iOS and Android components were slightly different due to platform constraints, the functionality stayed the same and did not impact user interaction or education.

Structure

The swipe indication pill and next flyer peek made the presence of adjacent flyers more visible at the moment users needed the cue.

Interaction

The flyer peek education showed on every even-numbered session (2, 4, 6), began 1 second after a user landed on a flyer, ran for 1200ms, did not repeat within the same session, and stopped after the sixth session.

Polish

The swipe indication pill appeared on every session without stopping, reinforcing the interaction pattern over time while keeping behavior consistent across platforms.

Outcome

After a 3-month experiment with a small test cohort, the team validated that targeted UX changes were enough to improve cross-browse adoption without a full redesign before rolling the experience out to all users.

  • 📈

    Adoption grew

    The test showed that the new UX changes led to a significant increase in cross-browse adoption (+8.92%).

  • 🛒

    Ad engagement improved

    Premium cross-browse ad clicks per user increased by 43.83%, and overall premium ad clicks per user increased by 13.14%.

  • 🚀

    Rolled out broadly

    The experience rolled out to 100% of users, with cross-browse adoption continuing to grow.

Explorations

Explorations tested different ways to communicate swipe affordance, preview adjacent flyers, and balance user education. The final direction focused on cues that clarified the gesture without overwhelming the flyer content.
