Replaced legacy banners with shoppable ads

Completed while at Flipp

Overview

In-App Collection Ads transformed Flipp's ad experience by replacing legacy, static Google Ad Manager (GAM) banners with a dynamic, video or display unit paired with an interactive shoppable carousel.

As the lead product designer for this initiative, I led the end-to-end design strategy across two high-traffic app surfaces: Search and Browse. My focus was on driving higher ad engagement and CPM (Cost Per Impression) value for brand partners while establishing strict UX guardrails to protect core flyer browsing, user retention, and overall app health.

What is Flipp?

Flipp is a leading deal-discovery platform that helps millions of shoppers plan weekly grocery and retail purchases through digital flyers, search, and personalized deal feeds from local retailers.

Problem

Flipp needed to move beyond legacy static Google Ad Manager (GAM) banners. They were reliable for revenue, but engagement was weak, with a baseline CTR (Click-through Rate) around 0.29%, and they couldn't showcase actionable, item-level deals directly inside the feed.

The core design challenge was introducing high-impact, shoppable ads into Search and Browse without degrading flyer browsing, session depth, or retention. On Browse especially, new ad units would compete for feed real estate alongside premium flyer tiles, the product experience users come to Flipp for.

Goals

  • Business: Increase ad engagement and CPM (Cost Per Impression) value for brand partners through a native video and shoppable carousel format.
  • User: Protect core flyer browsing and retention; ads should feel integrated into the feed, not disruptive to it.
  • Design: Ship a cohesive shoppable ad experience across Search (high-intent) and Browse (discovery-first).
  • Guardrails: Define ad system rules for slot placement, density, video behavior, and carousel interactions before scaling into high-traffic feed positions.

The solution

Collection Ads replace static banners with dynamic, in-feed units billed on a CPM (Cost Per Impression) basis and built around a shoppable carousel. Two format variants define the system: the standard format, pairing video or display with a 1–3 item carousel, and the compact format, a reduced-footprint variant currently in testing.

The standard format launched across two primary surfaces:

  • Search: Positioned at slots 3 and 7 within Search results. Targeted by exact, competitive, and related keywords, this surface served high-intent shoppers and proved stable early on.
  • Browse: Placed within high-value feed slots (slots 4–9), competing directly for real estate alongside Flipp's primary content: premium flyer tiles.
  • Validation: Each slot was tested to align with user expectations, protect core flyer browsing and retention, and support overall app health while still advancing business and revenue goals.

User research

Because ads would ship into feed slots competing with core flyer content, we couldn't scale on intuition alone. Before launching across Search and Browse, Design and Research ran 7 unmoderated rounds via UserTesting.com with 84 participants (casual, power, and browser personas) to validate engagement without harming retention.

We tested video controls, item-card interactions, carousel card density, and alternative IAB formats (250×250 and 300×250).

Outcome

  • 📈

    A native ad product replaced legacy banners

    Collection Ads moved Flipp from static GAM banners to a CPM (Cost Per Impression)-billed, in-feed format with video or display plus a shoppable 1–3 item carousel, giving brand partners item-level storytelling the legacy unit couldn't support.

  • 🔍

    Search launched and scaled first

    Search placements at slots 3 and 7 shipped ahead of Browse and proved stable with high-intent shoppers, creating a validated foundation before expanding into the more sensitive Browse feed.

  • 🛡️

    Research-backed guardrails shipped

    Seven rounds of testing across 84 participants codified core rules: muted video by default, carousel density limits, specific slot placement, and tighter Browse footprints to reduce feed disruption.

  • 🛒

    Shoppable carousel drove engagement

    Users interacted with the carousel when deals were visually clear: red pricing and sale cues made the unit feel relevant rather than purely promotional, aligning ad value with Flipp's deal-discovery mission.

  • 🧭

    Browse expanded with retention guardrails intact

    Browse rolled out to high-value feed slots (4–9) with stricter visual and placement standards informed by Visual Impact and format testing, balancing monetization in direct competition with premium flyer tiles while keeping the feed usable for core shopping behavior.

Production results

Standard format · ~2.3M users per A/B test. Compact format in testing, not included.

  • 🔍

    Search delivered accretive lift

    Brand campaigns drove +71% ARPU with a monitored ~3% dip in premium flyer clicks. Carousel CTR reached ≈0.6%, the benchmark Browse was expected to beat.

  • 🛒

    Browse Test 2 lifted engagement

    Lifts of +27.7% clicks/user, CTR +32%, and +108% carousel + FBP (Flipp Branded Page) CTR landed alongside near-flat retention guardrails. Viewability at 29% vs. 44% control blocked a broad rollout.

  • 📊

    Two surfaces, two paths forward

    Search has been accretive with retention guardrails holding steady under monitoring. Browse delivered strong engagement with near-flat retention metrics, and compact format testing is building on those results to refine footprint and unlock broader rollout.

Design

Research findings directly shaped the standard format's shipped UI, including slot placement, video behavior, carousel density, and surface-specific layouts for Search and Browse. Each variant was designed to feel native in-feed while making deals scannable at a glance.

On Search, the unit paired video or display with a shoppable carousel beneath, optimized for high-intent keyword context at slots 3 and 7. Browse used a tighter footprint for slots 4–9, with item cards tuned for faster scanning so the ad didn't compete visually with premium flyer tiles.

  • Video: Autoplay muted by default, with explicit controls, addressing the top retention concern from testing.
  • Carousel: 1–3 item slots with sale-forward pricing (red price, deal cues) to match how users already scan Flipp feeds.
  • Guardrails: Sponsored labeling, density caps, and surface-specific slot rules codified into the ad system before Browse scaled.

Explorations

Iterations tested item card sizes, video layouts, and full feed compositions across Search and Browse, from one to three item slots and alternate IAB formats to complete layout shifts. Research settled on the standard format: a native in-feed unit with a shoppable carousel, muted video, and surface-specific Search and Browse treatments. A compact format is now in testing separately to address Browse footprint and viewability gaps.

Reflections

  • Research only drives decisions when the team is educated on it: translating findings into clear recommendations for product, media science, and engineering helped research shape slot rules, video behavior, and format calls instead of sitting in a report.
  • Ad guidelines are the daily defense of user experience: documented standards for muted video, density limits, and slot placement gave Design a consistent way to evaluate new asks and protect flyer browsing as Collection Ads expanded through Browse.
  • A real beta would have opened more room to experiment: unmoderated testing answered directional questions, but iterating in production feed slots carried revenue and retention risk. A controlled beta would have allowed bolder exploration first.
  • Replacing reliable revenue created real product tension: GAM banners were predictable but low-performing. Collection Ads promised higher CPM, but had to prove itself in live slots without hurting retention, balancing monetization pressure with strict UX guardrails.
